Most people develop small routines without paying much attention to them. A morning may begin with opening a window, making coffee, checking the weather, and deciding what to wear. None of these actions requires much thought because experience has made them familiar. The same thing happens when learning a physical skill. At first every movement requires attention, but gradually several separate actions begin to work together. Progress can feel slow during this stage even when useful changes are happening. Regular practice gives the brain enough experience to recognize common situations and respond without waiting for a conscious decision each time.
